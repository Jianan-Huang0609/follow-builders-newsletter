Theme 01

The Automation Paradox / 自动化悖论

Every 的实践经验：全面采用 AI agent 之后不是裁员，是扩张——而且是有意为之的扩张。

DS
Dan Shipper / Nathan Baschez (Every)
Every 团队播客
中文

Every 团队——那个办了知名 AI 通讯的媒体公司——分享了一个反直觉的结果：在用 AI 激进地自动化了工作流之后，他们不但没有裁员，员工数反而翻了三倍。核心洞察来自 Dan Shipper 的文章《After Automation》：AI 让「昨天的专家能力」变得廉价。因为 AI 是用过去的产出训练的，昨天还需要专家做的事情，今天就成了白菜价。

这产生了一个连锁反应：专家级工作民主化之后，人人都开始做。但 AI 训练的是昨天的数据，所以默认 prompt 产出的东西看起来都差不多。竞争优势从「把事做好」变成了「做模型没见过的新东西」——而这需要人，更多的人，去做更野心勃勃的事。

团队的操作法则：「agent 离人越远，价值越低。」AI 最好用的时候，是作为人类专业能力的放大器，而不是替代品。你需要专家来打底、精准 prompt 控制方向、把关输出并推向新方向。

他们还给了最近听过最干净的 AGI 定义：「任何一个你从不关掉——而且关掉它在经济上不划算——的 agent。」按这个标准，今天很多开发者的 coding agent 已经达标了。

AI在工作场所的逻辑：AI让昨天的专家能力变得廉价。AI用我们所有的产出训练而成，能以极低成本做到昨天的专家水平。

agent离人越远，价值越低。

AGI的好定义：任何一个你从不关掉、关掉它在经济上不划算的agent。

即使有疯狂的新技术可用，变化速度也比你想象的要慢得多。

English

The team at Every, the media company behind the popular AI newsletter, shares a counterintuitive result: after aggressively automating their workflows with AI, they didn't shrink—they tripled their headcount. The core insight comes from Dan Shipper's essay 'After Automation': AI makes 'yesterday's expert competence cheap.' Because AI is trained on past outputs, whatever was expert-level work yesterday becomes a commodity today.

This creates a cascade: as expert work gets democratized, everyone starts doing it. But because AI is trained on yesterday's data, the outputs from default prompts all look similar. The competitive advantage shifts from 'doing the thing' to 'doing something new that the model hasn't seen yet.' And that requires humans—more humans, doing more ambitious things.

The team's operational rule: 'The further away an agent gets from a human, the less valuable it is.' AI works best as an amplifier for human expertise, not a replacement. You need experts to lay the groundwork, prompt the agents effectively, and interpret their outputs in novel directions.

They also gave one of the cleanest definitions of AGI heard in a while: 'Any agent that you never turn off, that it makes economic sense to keep running all the time.' By that measure, today's coding agents are already there for many developers.

The way that AI works in the workplace is AI makes yesterday's expert competence cheap. AI is trained on all of our outputs, all of the code and the writing and the design, and it can do things at that level at a very cheap price.

The further away an agent gets from a human, the less valuable it is.

A good definition of AGI is any agent that you never turn off, that it makes economic sense to keep it running all the time.

The rate of change, even when crazy new technology is available, is actually a lot slower than you would expect.
