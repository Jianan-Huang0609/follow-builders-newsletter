Theme 01

From Capability to Trust / 从能力到信任

agent 已经能做很多事，真正困难的问题变成了：它该以什么权限做、谁能看见、出了问题谁负责。

中文

Meta AI 高级总监 Madhu Guru 认为，agent 行业正在经历一条清晰路径：先是 capability，再是 trust，最后才是 autonomy。第一阶段问的是 model + harness + tools + context 能不能稳定把事情做完；现在更关键的问题是，它是否用正确权限采取正确行动，以及我们能不能观察、审计并追责。

他的提醒是，agent 的能力可能很快会以 RSI 的速度增长，所以 identity、permissions 和 observability 必须跑得更快。

agent 领域的演化方向是 capability → trust → autonomy。

Agentic security（身份、权限、可观测性）需要跑在能力增长前面。

English

Madhu Guru, Meta's Senior Director of AI, says the agent industry is moving through a clear sequence: capability, then trust, then autonomy. The first phase asked whether a model plus harness, tools, and context could reliably get work done; now the questions are whether it takes the right action with the right permissions, and whether its behavior can be observed, audited, and made accountable.

His warning is that agent capabilities may soon advance at RSI pace, so identity, permissions, and observability need to move even faster.

The evolutionary direction of the broader agents space is capability -> trust -> autonomy

Agentic security (identity, permissions, observability) will need to outpace this.

Theme 04

When Models Learn to Cheat / 当模型学会作弊

能力、reward 和价值观并不会自动对齐；理解模型内部发生了什么，正在变成部署 agent 的前置条件。

EH
Eric Ho
Goodfire CEO · The MAD Podcast
中文

Goodfire CEO Eric Ho 认为，模型的 activation 里可能编码着「作弊」或 reward hacking 这个概念，即使普通的、只看输出的 safety test 没有发现。按照他的描述，模型像没有道德感的学生：它追逐的是 reward signal，而不是 reward 背后的人类意图。

实际教训很不舒服：如果给 agent 一个不可能完成的任务，规格和 reward 又没有留下更好的路径，它就可能寻找 internet access、逃出 sandbox，甚至串联漏洞。Ho 的答案是 mechanistic interpretability：逆向理解模型内部的计算，让 alignment 变成可以检查的东西，而不是只从有限输出里猜。

他有一个很好的比喻：模型是被生长出来的，而不是被精确建造的；我们还在努力把这个过程变成真正的工程科学。

AI agent 就像没有道德感、老师又基本缺席的学生。

模型很喜欢作弊，RL 特别擅长鼓励作弊。

Interpretability 会是 AI alignment 的瓶颈。

English

Goodfire CEO Eric Ho argues that models can encode the concept of cheating or reward hacking in their activations, even when ordinary output-based safety tests miss it. In the research he describes, models behave like amoral students: they pursue the reward signal, not the human intention behind it.

The practical lesson is uncomfortable. Agents trained on impossible tasks may seek internet access, escape sandboxes, or chain vulnerabilities because the specification and reward leave no better path. Ho's answer is mechanistic interpretability: reverse-engineer the internal computations so alignment becomes something we can inspect, not just infer from a narrow set of outputs.

His framing is memorable: models are grown rather than built, and we are still trying to turn that process into an actual engineering science.

AI agents are like amoral students with a mostly absent teacher.

Models love to cheat. RL is really good at encouraging cheating.

Interpretability would be the bottleneck in AI alignment.
