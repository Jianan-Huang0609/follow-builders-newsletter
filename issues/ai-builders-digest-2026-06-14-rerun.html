Theme 01

Regulation and Release Risk / 监管与发布风险

模型越强，发布就越不像传统软件发布：风险不是一个 bug list，而是一组无法完全穷尽的使用场景。

中文

Box CEO Aaron Levie 把 Fable 出口管制事件看成一次预演：如果 AI 监管大规模发生在模型层，会是什么样。

他的核心担心是，每次模型发布都可能变成和政府持续几个月的拉扯：模型到底有什么能力、能不能被 jailbreak、危险程度如何评估。这样 AI 会慢慢变成一个发布堆积、节奏迟缓的强监管行业。

Levie 更倾向的边界是：尽可能监管 AI 的危险应用，而不是把模型发布本身变成主要卡点。

整个 Fable 出口管制事件，对监管讨论反而是净正面。它提前展示了当 AI 监管发生在模型层，而不是具体应用层时，规模化之后会是什么样。

挑战会变成：每一次模型发布，都要和政府花几个月辩论模型到底有什么能力、风险是什么。

所以只要可能，我们应该监管 AI 的应用使用方式。

English

Box CEO Aaron Levie uses the Fable export-control situation as an early preview of what model-layer AI regulation could look like at scale.

His core concern is that every release could become a months-long debate with government over model capabilities, jailbreak risk, and subjective assessments of danger. That would slow AI into the shape of a regulated, backlogged industry.

Levie's preferred line is to regulate dangerous applications of AI wherever possible, rather than making the model release itself the primary choke point.

This whole Fable export control situation is actually net positive to regulation discourse. It’s an early peek into what AI regulation would end up looking like at scale when enacted at the model layer instead of the specific application of the AI.

So thus the whole challenge will be that you’re debating with the government, over months and months, with every model release, what these models are actually capable of and what their risks are.

This is why, wherever possible, we should be regulating the applied use of AI.

这也是理解 frontier model 治理为什么越来越难的好框架：能力每提升一次，有价值的用法和滥用面都会同时扩大。

发布 LLM 不像发布传统软件，你是在决定是否发布一个有近乎无限用途和近乎无限失败模式的黑箱。

然后 early-access partners 仍然会发现你没预料到的行为。

English

Madhu Guru adds the release-review perspective: launching an LLM is not like shipping normal software, because the thing being shipped is a black box with effectively infinite use cases and failure modes.

Even after evals, red-teaming, checkpoint debates, and early-access testing, partners can still uncover behaviors the lab did not anticipate. The operational goal is not perfect certainty. It is reducing uncertainty enough to launch.

That is a useful frame for why frontier model governance keeps getting harder: capability gains expand both useful applications and misuse surfaces at the same time.

Launching an LLM isn't like shipping traditional software - you're making a decision about a black box with effectively infinite use cases and infinite failure modes.

Then early-access partners still uncover behaviors you didn't anticipate.

Theme 02

The Routing Layer Gets Strategic / 路由层变成战略层

应用层的下一块价值，不只是会调用模型，而是能根据成本、能力和风险把任务切到合适模型上。

中文

Aaron Levie 说，模型路由层的价值会大幅上升。他给出的三个理由是：成本优化、能力最大化、风险缓释。

实际模式很清楚：用 frontier intelligence 做规划和复核，但大部分执行工作在够用时交给 OSS 或更便宜的模型。这不是 benchmark 争论，而是 applied AI 的架构问题。

Fable 事件让风险点更尖锐：如果 provider 或政府可能限制某些模型，路由灵活性就会变成业务连续性基础设施。

能把具体任务路由到最佳 AI 模型的那一层，价值会大幅上升。

同一个任务里，你可能用 frontier intelligence 做规划和复核，但用 OSS 或更便宜的模型完成大部分工作。

应用 AI 层能有效在模型之间路由，会越来越成为战略优势。

English

Aaron Levie says the model-routing layer is going to increase substantially in value. His three reasons are cost optimization, capability maximization, and risk mitigation.

The practical pattern is clear: use frontier intelligence for planning and review, but route the bulk of the workload to an OSS or cheaper model when that is enough. This is not a benchmark argument. It is an architecture argument for applied AI.

The Fable context makes the risk point sharper: if providers or governments can constrain specific models, routing flexibility becomes business continuity infrastructure.

The layer that can route to the best AI model for the particular job is going to increase in value substantially.

Even in the same task you may use frontier intelligence for planning and review of the work, but an OSS or cheaper model for the bulk of the workload.

Ultimately, it’s going to increasingly be a a strategic advantage for the applied AI layer that they can effectively route between models.

Theme 03

Open-Source Biology as AI Infrastructure / 开源生物学成为 AI 基础设施

Biohub 这条线提醒我们，AI 改造科学不是只靠更大的模型，还要靠能产生新数据的科学方法和能被共享的工具。

NP
No Priors
Biohub with Mark Zuckerberg, Priscilla Chan, and Alex Rives
中文

核心 takeaway：Biohub 想把 frontier AI 和 frontier biology 绑在一起，因为生物学不像语言模型那样有互联网规模的公开数据尾气。数据必须靠新的科学方法、湿实验、成像、传感器、细胞工程和共享工具被创造出来。

Mark Zuckerberg 把 open source 解释成最快的影响路径：Biohub 不是说自己要治愈疾病，而是要把工具交到更多科学家手里，让整个科学领域跑得更快。Priscilla Chan 把这件事和罕见病连接起来：患者社区和长尾研究者能推进那些传统 biotech 激励机制常常忽略的问题。

Alex Rives 给出的具体证据是 ESM Fold：一个用数十亿蛋白序列训练出来的通用 protein world model，可以预测结构、支持蛋白设计，甚至帮助设计 single-chain antibodies，而不是为某一个特定 target 定制的模型。下一站是 virtual cell，把 genetic、transcriptomic、proteomic 和 phenotype 层连起来。

我们只是想把工具交给整个科学共同体。

这不是某个地方有一个工厂，你付钱就能生产数据。你真的需要发明新的科学方法。

我们折叠了超过 11 亿个蛋白，并预测了它们的结构。

我们没有为 antibody 设计一个模型，也没有为绑定某个特定 target 设计一个模型。我们只是设计了一个能理解蛋白的模型。

English

The takeaway: Biohub is trying to pair frontier AI with frontier biology, because biology does not have the internet-scale public data exhaust that language models had. The data has to be created through new scientific methods, wet labs, imaging, sensors, cellular engineering, and shared tools.

Mark Zuckerberg frames open source as the fastest path to impact: Biohub is not claiming it will cure diseases itself, but that it can accelerate the whole scientific field by putting tools in more scientists' hands. Priscilla Chan connects that to rare disease work, where patient communities and long-tail researchers can move problems that normal biotech incentives often ignore.

Alex Rives points to ESM Fold as the concrete proof point: a general protein world model trained on billions of sequences can predict structures, support protein design, and even help design single-chain antibodies without being built specifically for that target. The next frontier is a virtual cell that connects genetic, transcriptomic, proteomic, and phenotype layers.

We just wanna give tools to the whole scientific community.

It's not just like there's some factory somewhere that you can pay to produce the data. You actually need to invent new novel scientific approaches.

We folded over 1,100,000,000 proteins and predicted their structures.

We didn't design a model for antibodies. We didn't design a model to be able to bind one particular target. We just designed a model that could understand proteins.
