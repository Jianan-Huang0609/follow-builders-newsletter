Safety Is Moving Upstream / 安全正在前移

讨论重点从模型发布后的治理，转向能力增长发生之前的开发与评估。

SA
Sam Altman
@sama
中文

Sam Altman 认为，frontier lab 应该在可能显著提升能力的 reinforcement learning 运行前，先写出明确的 safety case。这里的 pacing 不是停止进步，而是接受监控和评估的成本，避免能力跑在 alignment 前面。

他希望行业共享 misalignment 与 monitoring 标准，并引入独立审计；国际协调则需要政府参与。

现在需要把重点转向安全开发与评估。

任何美国竞争压力，都不应该为鲁莽开发辩护，也不应该让能力领先于 alignment 和 monitoring。

English

Sam Altman argues that frontier labs should build safety cases before reinforcement-learning runs expected to increase capability. His definition of pacing is not stopping progress, but accepting the cost of monitoring and evaluation so capability does not outrun alignment.

He wants shared standards for misalignment and monitoring, plus independent auditors, while leaving international coordination to governments.

Today's shift to focusing on safe development and evaluation will need new tools.

Pacing will be well worth this cost; no amount of American competitive pressure should justify recklessness, or let capabilities get ahead of alignment and monitoring.

English

There are two ways AI progress could go very badly that we must avoid.

We could lose control of the future to AI, or end up in a world with too much concentration of power.

The exact chart may have moved since February, but the point survives: we are still very early.

Most of the world hasn't even started using it yet.

Theme 04

Compute Is a System / 算力是一整个系统

accelerator 不是全部，CPU、内存、供应链和机器人终端共同决定 AI 能走多远。

NP
No Priors
Arm CEO · No Priors
中文

Arm CEO Rene Haas 认为，AI 硬件绝不是只有 accelerator：CPU 仍然是系统的调度者，accelerator 负责生成 token，CPU 负责决定 token 去哪里。他还预计晶圆、内存和数据中心建设的供应链约束至少还会持续数年；等成本和商业模式成熟后，机器人会成为 ARM 的重要机会。

没有任何计算问题不需要、也不能使用 microprocessor。

token 工厂只是生成 token，真正把 token 送给用户的‘卡车’就是 CPU。

English

Arm CEO Rene Haas argues that AI hardware is not an accelerator-only story. CPUs remain the system's orchestrator: accelerators generate tokens, while CPUs decide where those tokens go. He also expects supply-chain constraints across wafers, memory, and data-center construction to remain for years, and sees robotics as a major ARM opportunity once costs and business models mature.

There's no computing problem that's ever been invented that doesn't utilize and can't utilize the microprocessor.

The token factory just generates all these tokens. It's like literally where are the trucks that are gonna take the tokens away and give them to the users? That's what CPUs do.
