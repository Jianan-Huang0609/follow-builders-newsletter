The Evals Goldilocks Zone / 评测的「金发姑娘」区间

评测正在从玄学变成工程：好的评测要分层、要自己从真实输出里搭出来，而不是只盯着最终答案。

中文

Meta 的 AI 高级总监 Madhu Guru 继续他的「如何构建好的评测」系列，第 7 部分提出了评测构建的金发姑娘原则：你的评测应该在各个「待完成的工作」层面去衡量，而不只是看最终答案。

他拿金融分析 agent 举例：大多数团队只会造一套标准答案，检查 agent 有没有推荐「正确」的股票。但这漏掉了所有有意义的中间环节——理解客户、收集证据、分析数据、然后才给出推荐。每个阶段都应该有自己的评测，这样当最终答案错了，你能立刻知道该去哪里排查：客户理解 92%、证据提取 92%、数据分析 70%、推荐 75%。

他的经验法则：「不能太粗，也不能太细，刚刚好。」把评测粒度做到你能诊断问题、采取行动的程度。

如何构建好的评测 - 第 7 部分。评测构建的金发姑娘原则：评测应该在各个待完成工作的层面去衡量，而不只是最终答案。

最常见的错误是团队造一套标准答案，检查 agent 有没有推荐「正确」的股票。

如果最终推荐错了，设计良好的评测集会告诉你：客户理解 92%、证据提取 92%、数据分析 70%、推荐 75%。现在你知道该去哪挖了。

不能太粗，也不能太细，刚刚好。把评测粒度做到你能诊断并行动的程度。

English

Meta's Sr Director of AI Madhu Guru continues his 'How to build great evals' series with part 7, laying out a Goldilocks principle for eval construction: your evals should measure at the level of each job to be done, not just the final answer.

His example is a financial analysis agent. Most teams just create a golden set of right answers and check whether the agent recommended the 'right' stock. But that misses all the meaningful intermediate jobs — understanding the client, gathering evidence, analyzing data, then making the recommendation. Each stage should have its own eval, so a wrong final answer tells you exactly where to dig: client understanding 92%, evidence extraction 92%, data analysis 70%, recommendation 75%.

His rule of thumb: 'Not too granular. Not too coarse. Just right.' Make your eval set as granular as you need to diagnose and act.

How to build great evals - part 7. The Goldilocks principle for eval construction. Your evals should measure at the level of the various jobs to be done, not just the final answer.

The most common mistake I see is teams create a golden set of right answers and check if the agent recommended the "right" stock.

If the final recommendation is wrong, a well designed eval set would tell you: Client understanding : 92%, Evidence extraction : 92%, Data analysis: 70%, Recommendation: 75%. Now you know where to go dig.

Peter Yang 转述了评测专家 Shreya Shankar 的一个实用心智模型：AI 评测分两种——自上而下和自下而上。

自上而下是：把你放在真空里，只给你任务描述，你会想出什么？Claude 非常擅长帮你做这种自上而下的评测。自下而上是另一半：当你看了大量样本输出，你想把什么样的直觉反馈外化成评测？这部分，Shankar 说，全靠你自己——Claude 在生成自下而上的评测上「非常非常差」。

这跟 Madhu Guru 当天发的帖子正好呼应：最难、最有价值的评测，恰恰是那些你必须从真实输出里自己搭出来的。

AI 评测有两种：自上而下和自下而上。

自上而下：在真空里只给任务描述，你会想到什么？Claude 很擅长这种自上而下的评测。

自下而上是另一半：看大量样本输出时，你想把什么直觉反馈外化成评测？Claude 做自下而上评测非常非常差，那全得靠你。

English

Peter Yang surfaces a useful mental model from evals expert Shreya Shankar: there are two kinds of AI evals — top-down and bottom-up.

Top-down means, given just a task description in a vacuum, what would you come up with? Claude is very good at helping with these. Bottom-up is the other half: looking at lots and lots of sample outputs and externalizing your gut feedback into evals. That part, Shankar says, is all you — Claude is 'very, very bad' at coming up with bottom-up evals.

It pairs neatly with Madhu Guru's post from the same day: the hardest, most valuable evals are the ones you have to build yourself from real outputs.

There are two types of AI evals - tops down and bottoms up.

Think of top-down as: If you're in a vacuum, just given the task description, what would you come up with? Claude does a very good job of helping you with these top-down evals.

Bottom-up evals are the other half. When you look at lots and lots of sample outputs, what is your gut feedback that you want externalized into evals? Claude is very, very bad at coming up with bottom-up evals. That's all you.
