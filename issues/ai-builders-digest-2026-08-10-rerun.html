当 agent 开始互相通信、共享知识，这既可能是安全噩梦，也可能是新的公共品——有人在认真赌后者。

Theme 03

Enterprise Diffusion / 企业落地的真实节奏

不是所有工作都像写代码一样适合 agent——扩散注定不均衡，而 Linear 展示了一种让 agent 自己推动产品改进的机制。

中文

Box CEO Aaron Levie 解释了为什么 agent 在企业里的扩散注定不均衡：agentic coding 之所以垂直爆发，是因为它是一种连续不间断的电脑工作——经济价值直接和纯数字产出挂钩，单次会话的任务规模理论上没有上限。大多数工作天生不具备这个属性：销售要等客户反馈才能推进，律师要跟客户谈，医生要面对病人。

他的核心观点是：在多数领域，agent 不会嵌入现有的工作流，工作流本身必须为 AI 重新设计。他引用 Factory 的 Matan Grinberg 在 Training Data 播客上的说法：如果明天所有人请病假，token 用量会暴跌——因为几乎还没有 agent 在后台自动运行。而未来的量就在那里：法律领域处理每一份进来的合同，销售领域 agent 在客户记录里巡游找触达时机，生命科学领域成群的 agent 读遍每一份测试和研究。

他说这个大机会伴随的是「大量变革管理、大量数据清理、大量工作流重构」——比写代码慢得多、乱得多，但可能是更大的市场。

agent 在企业里扩散速度会不均衡的一个原因：不同的工作流对「连续、不间断的电脑工作」的适配程度不一样。

Agentic coding 之所以完全垂直增长，一个很大的原因是：这种工作的经济价值与纯数字产出直接相关，而且单次会话里的任务规模理论上可以无上限。这意味着，随着 agent 单次能干更多活、模型在这类任务上越来越强，它们几乎可以瞬时被部署到更大的工作量上。

很多工作在今天的工作流设计里天生不具备这个属性。销售要对客户有反馈循环才能继续推进某个客户的活，律师要跟客户沟通，医生要跟病人互动。所以至少在默认情况下，大规模的 agentic 工作不会像写代码那样发生。相反，在这些领域，agent 需要接入一个为 AI 重新设计的工作流。

Factory 的 Matan Grinberg 在 Training Data 播客上有个很好的说法：如果明天所有人都请病假，token 用量会暴跌——因为没人去 prompt 它们了。这说明在大多数流程里，agent 的后台自动运行还处于极早期；而这本该是未来 agentic 工作量的主体。

法律领域是处理每一份进来的合同；销售领域是 agent 在客户记录里巡游、找出更好的触达时机；生命科学研究领域是成群的 agent 读遍每一项测试和所有研究。

要做到这些，业务流程必须改造得能支撑 agent，而不是像今天这样靠海量的人工。这就是现在的大机会，但它的扩散方式和 agentic coding 完全不同：大量变革管理、大量数据清理、大量工作流重构，等等。

English

Box CEO Aaron Levie explains why agent adoption in the enterprise will be uneven: agentic coding went vertical because it's continuous, uninterrupted computer work where economic value scales with digital output and task size is unbounded in a single session. Most other work doesn't natively have that property — a sales rep needs a customer feedback loop, a lawyer needs to talk to the client, a doctor needs the patient.

His point: in most domains, agents won't slot into existing workflows; the workflows themselves have to be reengineered for AI. He cites Factory's Matan Grinberg: if everyone called in sick tomorrow, token usage would plummet — because almost no agents run in the background yet. That's where the future volume is: legal processing every contract, sales agents roaming customer records for outreach signals, life-science swarms reading every test.

The big opportunity, he says, comes with "lots of change management, lots of data cleanup, lots of workflow reengineering" — slower and messier than coding, but arguably the bigger market.

One reason why we're going to get uneven diffusion rates of agents is because different workflows in the enterprise are more or less aligned to continuous, uninterrupted computer work.

A big reason why agentic coding growth has gone completely vertical is because it's a type of work where economic value is directly correlated with the output of solely digital information, and where the task size can in theory be unbounded in a single session. This means that as AI agents can do larger amounts of work at a time, and as model capability improves on this task type, they can directly be deployed at larger workloads near instantaneously.

Lots of work doesn't natively have this property in how today's workflows are designed. A sales rep needs a feedback loop with the customer before they can do additional work for a specific account, a lawyer needs to talk to the client, and a doctor needs to interact with a patient. So large agentic work will likely not look the same as it has in coding, at least by default. Instead, in many of these domains, the agents will actually need to tie into a workflow that is reengineered for AI.

Matan Grinberg at Factory had a good way of thinking about this on the Training Data podcast: if everyone called in sick tomorrow, token usage would plummet because no one would be there to prompt them. This shows how early we are in agents running in the background for most processes; whereas this should be the majority of volume of agentic work in the future.

In legal, it will be about processing every contract that comes in, in sales it will be agents that roam through customer records and figure out signals of when to better do outreach, in life sciences research it will be about swarms of agents reading through every test or all research.

To do that these business processes have to be wired up to support agents, as opposed to the vast amounts of manual work that we do today. This is the big opportunity right now, but it's going to diffuse differently from something like agentic coding. Lots of change management, lots of data cleanup, lots of workflow reengineering, and more.

The Craft of Building / 建造者的手艺

从根因出发修东西，以及为什么在 agent 时代你仍然应该读代码——判断力依然是稀缺品。

GT
Garry Tan
@garrytan
中文

Y Combinator 总裁兼 CEO Garry Tan 分享了他最喜欢的工作方式——这同时是一份 debugger 宣言：从 bug 出发，从缺口出发，从错误的说法出发，从半成品工具出发，从机构里奇怪的行为出发。然后问：什么样的隐藏机制，才让这个可见的失败成为可能？然后修复根因。永远重复。

我最喜欢的工作方式：

Garry Tan, President & CEO of Y Combinator, shares his favorite way to work on things — and it doubles as a debugger's manifesto: start from the bug, the gap, the false claim, the half-built tool, the weird behavior in the institution. English

Igor Babushkin — xAI co-founder, previously DeepMind (StarCraft, AlphaCode) and early reasoning work at OpenAI — explains on Unsupervised Learning why he left to start River AI: he got impatient watching power over AI concentrate. His diagnosis: the current distribution model — giant pretraining runs, an API, per-token pricing — isn't the only way, and helping individuals feel in control of AI is, to him, the most urgent part of AI safety.

River is running three bets at once: the River API (a cheap, reliable reinforcement-learning and fine-tuning service), personal AI (models aligned to each individual instead of the average user — "how do you maximize somebody's happiness? That's the reward function"), and local hardware (can you run a frontier model on a device in your home, for latency, privacy, and control).

He also frames the current moment beautifully: the December when coding agents became impossible to ignore was a modern Sorcerer's Apprentice story — "we're all becoming the sorcerer's apprentices now." His bet is that the next manifestation of agents isn't frontier reasoning but everyday personal AI that knows you, your taste, and your day.

the coding agents suddenly became so powerful that you can't ignore them. Previously, some people used them, some people didn't use them, but from that point onwards, that latest iteration of Cloud Opus models, suddenly everybody agreed to, yes these models are very powerful. Yes they make our jobs much much easier.

So that's the first bet that we're making on the River API. The second one is around personal AI and personalization. So we feel like there's an opportunity to align AI agents much much better with the individual than what we're currently doing.

And then the third big bet that we're making is on hardware. Because right now the assumption is that the only way to get access to the most powerful agents is through access to a data center.

So how do you maximize somebody's happiness? That's the reward function. That hasn't really been done yet. So for me that is the key aspect.
