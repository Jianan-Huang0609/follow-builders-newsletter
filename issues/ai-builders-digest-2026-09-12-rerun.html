Theme 03

The Human-in-the-Loop Question / 人类仍在回路中

真正的自动化不是把人从流程里抹掉，而是重新定义需求、检查和组织责任由谁承担。

中文

Peter Yang 对“software factory”保持怀疑：除验证和测试外，AI 还不能在没有人定义需求、检查结果的情况下端到端做出产品或功能。他的反对理由很实际：只要开头有一个错误假设，跑一夜花掉的 token 可能全都白费。

我对“software factory”持怀疑态度。 除了验证和测试，我不认为 AI 已经能在没有人参与的情况下，自我改进产品或端到端构建新功能。 每当我让它通宵构建新东西，只要它做了一个错误假设，整晚的 token 就全浪费了。 有哪些产品或功能，是由 software factory 在没有人定义需求或检查工作的情况下端到端构建出来的？

English

Peter Yang is skeptical of software factories that claim to build products end-to-end without a human defining requirements or checking the work. His practical objection is the compounding cost of one wrong assumption: an overnight run can spend a pile of tokens producing something nobody wanted.

I am skeptical of "software factories." Other than for verification & testing, I don't think AI is at a point where it can self-improve a product or build a new feature end-to-end without human in the loop. Whenever I loop something overnight to build something new = if it makes one wrong assumption the whole thing becomes a waste of tokens. What products or features have been built end-to-end with a software factory and no human defining the requirements or checking the work?

The Agentic Economy / Agentic Economy

当 agent 不只是替人操作，而是拥有自己的账户和支付需求，软件边界会延伸到金融基础设施。

NP
No Priors
Coinbase cofounder and CEO
中文

核心结论：如果 agent 要独立完成交易，就需要为小额、高频支付设计的金融基础设施。Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong 指出，银行卡费用在 1 美元以下就不划算，而他们看到的 agent 电商交易约 76% 低于 30 美分。 他的方向是 agentic finance：让 agent 拥有自己的金融账户，并通过一条 prompt 激活简单工具。真正值得关注的不是“agent 会不会比人多”，而是背后的基础设施问题：一个能行动却不能付款、持有资金或转移价值的 agent，在关键边界上仍然离不开人。 “我们不希望 AI 处于未银行化状态。”

借记卡或信用卡交易通常至少有 30 美分固定费用，外加额外比例费用。对 1 美元以下的交易来说，这显然不太行；给 1 美分付款却要付 30 美分也没有意义。我们上次看到的统计是，agent 电商交易大约 76% 低于 30 美分。我们不希望 AI 处于未银行化状态。 AI agent 自己将需要拥有账户。

English

The Takeaway: if agents are going to transact independently, they need financial primitives designed for tiny, frequent payments. Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong points out that card economics break down below a dollar, while roughly 76% of the agent ecommerce transactions they saw were under 30 cents. His proposed direction is agentic finance: give agents their own financial accounts and simple tools they can activate with a prompt. The interesting part is not the prediction that there may be more agents than humans; it is the infrastructure question underneath it. An agent that can act but cannot pay, hold money, or move value is still dependent on a human at every meaningful boundary. "We don't want the AIs to be unbanked."

The minimum fee typically on a debit or credit card transaction is about 30¢ fee flat plus the additional percentage. Arguably, it doesn't really work well for anything under a dollar. Certainly, it doesn't make sense to pay 30¢ to send a 1¢ payment. And I believe the stats we last saw on this are that about, like, 76% of the agent ecommerce transactions we're seeing are under 30¢. We don't want the AIs to be unbanked. The AI agents themselves are gonna have to have their own accounts.
