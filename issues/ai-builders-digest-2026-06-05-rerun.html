AI 编程的边界与未来 / Coding's Automation Frontier

Aaron Levie 长文论证编程是 AI 自动化最理想场景——即便如此人类工程师仍供不应求。Ryo Lu 展示 Cursor 中「设计即代码」的新范式。

中文

Box CEO Aaron Levie 写了当天分析力最强的长帖（283 赞）。核心论点：编程基本上是 AI 自动化中最理想的目标场景——模型在海量高质量代码上训练、用户高度技术化、输出可验证（跑一下就知道对不对）、上下文已数字化在代码库中、代码质量与产品结果甚至经常脱耦（烂代码也能跑出好产品）。每一项都是 AI agent 梦寐以求的条件。

然而，即便拥有所有这些优势，人类工程师仍然供不应求。他的结论：「如果连编程在这种条件下都需要人类工程师大量参与，那其他知识工作领域的自动化风险只会被高估。Agent 会让人们比过去做更多事，但人不会消失。」这不是泼冷水，是把期望值校准到一个清醒的位置。如果最理想的自动化目标仍需人类监督，其他领域只会更难。

编程基本上是你能用 AI 合理自动化的终极场景，但我们仍然需要人类工程师来监督 agent 才能让它们有效。AI 模型在海量复杂代码上训练。用户高度技术化，能快速使用最新工具。工作成果可验证——你可以测试应用。结果往往与代码质量脱钩（烂代码也能跑出好产品）。agent 的上下文往往已经数字化在代码库中。

AI 编码 agent 享有大量优势。其中一些适用于知识工作，但大多数不适用——尤其是那些需要全面审阅才有用的工作，或数据没那么丰富的领域。这让 agent 在知识工作中更复杂。所以如果连这种条件下工程师都还很抢手，其他知识工作的风险只会比感知到的更低。Agent 会让人们做比以前更多的事，但人不会消失。

English

Box CEO Aaron Levie wrote the day's most analytically rigorous thread (283 likes). His core argument: coding is essentially the best-case scenario for AI automation — the models are trained on enormous amounts of sophisticated code, the users are highly technical, the output is verifiable (you can test the app), the context is already digitized in the codebase, and the outcome quality is often decoupled from code quality (sloppy code can still ship a working app).

And yet, with all of those advantages, human engineers remain in very high demand. His conclusion: 'If with all of that, engineers still remain in very high demand, the risks are going to be less than what's perceived for other areas of knowledge work. Agents will let people do far more than they did before, but the people don't go away.' It's a sobering calibration for anyone who's been forecasting mass displacement of knowledge workers. If coding — the ideal automation target — still needs human oversight, everything else needs it more.

Coding is basically the pinnacle of what you could reasonably automate with AI, and yet we still need human engineers to oversee agents for them to be effective. The AI models are trained on an incredible amount of sophisticated code. The users are highly technical and can use the latest tools quickly. The work is 'verifiable' because you can test an app. The outcomes are often removed from the quality of the code (you can have sloppy code but the app can still work). And the context for the agent is often already digitized and sitting in the codebase.

That's an incredible amount of benefits that AI coding agents get to work with. Some of those apply to knowledge work, but most don't in areas where the work needs to be fully reviewed to be useful, or where data isn't as abundantly digitized. This makes the job for agents in knowledge work more complicated. So if with all of that, engineers still remain in very high demand, the risks are going to be less than what's perceived for other areas of knowledge work. Agents will let people do far more than they did before, but the people don't go away.

工程方法论 / Engineering Practices

Swyx 提出「把任务框定为问题」的 prompt 技巧。Peter Yang 系统化分享可自我检查、自我进化的 AI skill 构建方法论。

中文

Swyx 给越来越流行的「始终使用 plan mode」建议提出了一个更聪明的替代方案：把任务框定为问题，这样模型会被邀请去质疑和评估想法质量、提出替代方案，而不是盲目执行你「说」的——你说的往往不是你真正「想」的。

这个建议的优雅之处在于它的极简：「在 prompt 末尾加个问号往往就够了。」一个字符的改变，把模型从执行模式切换到了咨询模式。在这个人人都忙着构建复杂多步 prompt 框架的时代，Swyx 在提醒我们：有时候最高杠杆的干预手段是语法层面的。

一个比「始终使用 plan mode」更聪明的替代方案：始终把任务框定为问题，这样模型会被邀请去质疑和评估想法质量、提出替代方案，而不是盲目执行你「说」要做的（这往往不是你真正「想」要的）。在 prompt 末尾加个问号往往就够了。

English

Swyx proposed a smarter alternative to the increasingly popular 'always use plan mode' advice: frame your task as a question, so the model is invited to push back and rate the quality of the idea or suggest alternatives, rather than blindly executing what you said — which is often not precisely what you meant.

The elegance of this advice is in its minimalism: 'Literally just appending "?" to the end of your prompt often does it.' It's a one-character change to your workflow that shifts the model from execution mode to consultation mode. In an era where everyone is building complex multi-step prompting frameworks, Swyx is reminding us that sometimes the highest-leverage intervention is grammatical.

中文

Peter Yang 发布了一套构建「可自我检查、自我进化」AI skill 的五步框架（154 赞）。步骤是刻意累积的：（1）给上下文——展示好的输出示例让 AI 内化质量标准。（2）让触发变简单——用标准化描述让 skill 易于调用。（3）加 evals——10 条常见错误的 pass/fail 清单。（4）加 memory——从过往对话中提取一句话经验持续记录。（5）元技能——构建一个能编辑其他 skill 的 skill，清理重复、过时指令、模糊规则和「AI 水话」。

第五步最有趣，因为它闭环了：一个维护其他 skill 的 skill 是最接近自进化 agent 的东西，而且不需要复杂的基础设施。这套框架刻意设计为在现有工具约束内运行——不需要向量数据库、不需要 RAG 流水线，只有 markdown 文件和 evals 清单。这是「无聊但有效」的 AI 工程流派，对大多数人来说可能比任何前沿 agent 架构都更有用。

如何构建可自我检查、自我进化的 AI skill：1. 给上下文——「为这个重复任务创建一个 skill。这里是好输出的示例，让它知道好是什么样子。」2. 让触发变简单——「用这个模式写一个清晰的 skill 描述：当用户想要做这个时使用。」3. 加 evals——「创建一个包含 10 个常见错误 pass/fail 检查的 evals md。」4. 加 memory——「创建一个 memory md 来捕获使用这个 skill 的过往对话中的一句话经验。」5. 构建编辑 skill 的 skill——「创建一个 skill 来清理其他 skill，移除重复或过时指令、模糊规则和 AI 水话。」

English

Peter Yang published a practical five-step framework for building AI skills that check their own work and improve over time (154 likes). The progression is deliberately cumulative: (1) Give the AI context by showing examples of good output so it internalizes quality standards. (2) Make skills easy to trigger with clear, standardized descriptions. (3) Add evals — a simple pass/fail checklist of 10 common errors. (4) Add memory — a running log of one-sentence learnings from past sessions. (5) Meta: build a skill that edits other skills — cleaning up duplicates, stale instructions, vague rules, and 'AI slop.'

Step 5 is the most interesting because it closes the loop: a skill that maintains other skills is the closest thing to a self-improving agent without requiring complex infrastructure. The framework is explicitly designed to work within current tool constraints — no vector databases, no RAG pipelines, just markdown files and evals checklists. It's the 'boring but effective' school of AI engineering, and it's probably more useful to more people than any cutting-edge agent architecture.

How to build AI skills that check their own work and improve over time: 1. Give it context: Ask AI 'Create a skill for this repeated task. Here are examples of good output so it knows what good looks like.' 2. Make it easy to trigger: 'Write a clear skill description using this pattern: Use when the user wants to do this.' 3. Add evals: 'Create an evals md with 10 pass/fail checks for common errors in the skill's output.' 4. Add memory: 'Create a memory md to capture one-sentence learnings from past chats using this skill.' 5. Build a skill to edit skills: 'Create a skill that cleans up other skills by removing duplicate or stale instructions, vague rules, and AI slop.'

主线：Figma 一直在重注投入开发者平台——插件、API、Dev Mode，以及现在的 AI 原生设计工具。Colyer 认为「技术的民主化」意味着会有巨量软件被建造出来，而提供画布、原语和分发的公司将捕获不成比例的价值。对谈覆盖了 Figma 如何把 AI 视为设计伙伴而非替代品、为什么设计工具中的开发者角色正变得比设计师角色更重要，以及为「人人都是开发者」的世界做产品意味着什么。

SaaS 末日，或者说软件的下一个时代。我对此非常兴奋，我想 Figma 和很多其他 SaaS 公司也是如此。我在开发者工具领域工作了很长时间，也许五到十年前，全球开发者数量的估计是 2,500 万、3,000 万、4,000 万左右。我觉得这个时代最令人兴奋的是，我认为这个数字会变成 10 亿，甚至更多。我们正在经历一个不可思议的产品开发和技术民主化时代。

我认为最终结果是世界上会有大量的软件。如果你在那个领域，这意味着它是一个金矿。

English

Dan Shipper sits down with Matt Colyer, Director of Product Management for Developers at Figma, for a conversation that reframes the 'SaaS apocalypse' narrative. Colyer's thesis: the collapse of traditional SaaS margins isn't a doom story — it's a goldmine for companies that understand the developer audience is about to explode from ~30 million to potentially a billion people who can build software.

The core thread: Figma has been investing heavily in its developer platform — plugins, APIs, the Dev Mode, and now AI-native design tools. Colyer argues that the 'democratization of technology' means dramatically more software gets built, and the companies that provide the canvas, the primitives, and the distribution will capture disproportionate value. The conversation covers how Figma thinks about AI as a design partner rather than a replacement, why the developer persona inside design tools is becoming more important than the designer persona, and what it means to build for a world where 'everyone is a developer.'

The SaaS apocalypse or the next era of software, if you will. I'm really excited about it, and I think Figma and a lot of other SaaS businesses are too. Because, like, I've worked in developer tools for a long time, and maybe five, ten years ago, the estimate of number of developers worldwide was like 25,000,000, 30,000,000, 40,000,000, give or take. I think what's most exciting about this time is that I think it's gonna be like a billion, maybe even more than that. I think there's this incredible time that we're moving through of product development and really the democratization of technology.

I think the end result is that there is dramatically more software out there in the world. If you're in that space, it means it's a gold mine.
