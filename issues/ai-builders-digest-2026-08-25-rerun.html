Software You Can Change With a Prompt / 可被 prompt 改写的软件

一个正在成形的 builder 共识：软件的价值，取决于你能否用一句话改掉它。

中文

Peter Steinberger（OpenClaw + OpenAI）把本周核心论点最直白地浓缩成了一句话：「我们必须远离那些无法用 prompt 改写的软件。」2423 个赞，显然戳中了很多人。

言外之意比字面更大：如果一个工具不能被自然语言重塑，它就被锁死在厂商交付的样子里——而在 agent 原生的工作流里，这是一种负担。软件正在从「你采用的固定产品」变成「你可以编辑的起点」。

我们必须远离那些无法用 prompt 改写的软件。

English

Peter Steinberger (OpenClaw + OpenAI) puts the bluntest version of the week's central thesis into a single line: 'We need to get away from software that we can't change with a prompt.' With 2423 likes, it clearly struck a chord.

The implication is bigger than it sounds: if a tool can't be reshaped by natural language, it's locked into whatever the vendor shipped — and in an agent-native workflow, that's a liability. Software is becoming a starting point you edit, not a fixed product you adopt.

We need to get away from software that we can't change with a prompt.

Discipline: Evals & Judgment / 判断纪律

模型再强，也得有办法衡量它、有办法迭代自己的判断——这两条讲的是 builder 的内功。

中文

Meta AI 的 Madhu Guru（此前在 Google 主导 Gemini、Veo、Nano Banana）给他的 evals 系列加上了第 8 篇，核心是一个犀利的概念：evals 的「区分力」。一个 eval 只有在能区分出真正不同的 AI 系统时才有用。

他的例子：跑五个系统，得分 94、93、95、94、92，但你其实知道 A 和 C 明显强于 D 和 E——这个 eval 区分力很低，就像拿五年级数学卷去考一群 PhD，人人都满分。他说最佳区间是「真实 + 有难度 + 对能力差异敏感」；而随着模型变强，好 eval 会逐渐饱和——这正是他留给评论区的问题。

evals 的区分力：爬山式 eval 只有在能区分真正不同的系统时才有用。跑五个系统 94/93/95/94/92，但已知 A、C 远强于 D、E，这个 eval 区分力太低。就像给 PhD 发五年级卷子，人人满分。最佳区间：真实+有难度+对能力差异敏感。

English

Meta AI's Madhu Guru (previously Google, where he led Gemini, Veo, and Nano Banana) adds part 8 to his evals series with a sharp concept: the 'discriminatory property' of evals. An eval is only useful if it can separate AI systems that are meaningfully different.

His example: run five systems and get 94, 93, 95, 94, 92 — but you already know A and C are substantially better than D and E. The eval has low discriminatory power; it's like giving fifth-graders a test where PhDs all ace it. The sweet spot, he says, is 'realistic + difficult + sensitive to differences in capability' — and over time good evals saturate as models improve, which is the problem he leaves open for the comments.

The discriminatory property of evals. A hill-climbing eval is useful when it can separate AI systems that are meaningfully different. Imagine you run an eval on five AI systems: A: 94 B: 93 C: 95 D: 94 E: 92. Now suppose you already know A and C are substantially better systems than D and E. The eval is bad cos it doesn't tell you that. It has low discriminatory power. The sweet spot for an eval is: Realistic + difficult + sensitive to differences in capability.
