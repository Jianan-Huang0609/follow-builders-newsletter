Agrawal 创立了 Parallel Web Systems，赌的是 agent 使用网络的次数会是人类的「一千倍」。他没有在第一天就跟 Google 的爬取+索引正面刚，而是先推出搜索 agent——查询到达后再去爬网页，用延迟换耐心，同时用真实的客户工作流（保险核保、销售数据补全、金融数据整理）来搞清楚 agentic 搜索到底该长什么样。

Evals & Applied AI at Scale / Eval 与应用层 AI 规模化

一边是 eval 纪律从「静态考题」进化成「活的路线图」，一边是应用层 AI 的战略窗口被重新确认。

中文

Madhu Guru 的「如何做高质量的 eval」系列到了第 9 篇，讲的是 Eval 路线图问题，也是目前最实操的一篇。他的核心论点是：大多数 eval 之所以失败，是因为团队把它当静态产物，而用户的期望和行为一直在变。

一个具体例子让论点落地：一个金融研究 agent，一开始用户要的是「总结这份 5 页财报」，几个月后就变成「监控我的投资组合，有变化就提醒我」。每个阶段需要不同的能力，因此需要不同的 eval。

他的路线图是一份 PM 101 清单：先画出用量会沿哪些维度演化（轮次、文档大小、工具使用、自主性），优先产品最在意的维度和场景，跟用户聊、挖生产日志找变化，为下一阶段搭 P0 eval，跑起来再针对失败模式爬山优化。金句在最后：如果你的 eval 停在第一周、用户已经在第三周，它会直接体现在你的产品和流失率上。

大多数 eval 失败，是因为团队把它们当静态产物，而用户的期望和行为已经变了。你的 eval 需要一张跟产品和实际用法一起演化的路线图。

短上下文→长上下文；单轮 QA→多轮；段落引用→文档和行级引用；简单问答→复杂综合；被动聊天→主动 agent。

English

Madhu Guru's "How to build great evals" series reaches Part 9 with the Eval Roadmap Problem, and it's the most practical entry yet. His thesis: most evals fail because teams treat them as static artifacts while their users' expectations and behaviors keep evolving.

The concrete example makes it land: a financial research agent starts with "summarize this 5-page earnings report," and a few months later users are asking it to "monitor my portfolio and alert me when something changes my thesis." Each stage needs different capabilities—and therefore different evals.

His roadmap is a PM 101 checklist: map the dimensions usage will evolve along (turns, document size, tool use, autonomy), prioritize what matters for your product, talk to users and mine production traces, build P0 evals for the next stage, then run them and hill-climb on failure modes. The punchline: if your evals are stuck in week 1 while users are in week 3, it shows up in your product and your churn.

Most evals fail because teams treat them as static artifacts while their users expectations and behaviors have evolved. Your evals need a roadmap that evolves with your product and actual usage patterns.

Google Labs shipped a new experiment called Play with Putty, a collaborative vibe-coding tool that lets people build tools and websites together in real time—"great minds play alike," the announcement puns. It's an early signal of where coding tools are heading: multiplayer.
