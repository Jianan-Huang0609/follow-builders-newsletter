Theme 01

The Stamina Dividend / 模型的耐力红利

当模型有花不完的耐心和几乎免费的算力，「没人会做的事」正在变成「顺手就做」。

AK
Andrej Karpathy
@karpathy
中文

OpenAI 联创、前特斯拉 AI 负责人 Andrej Karpathy 说，我们正在离开用「画一只骑自行车的鹈鹕 SVG」这种玩具题来测 LLM 的时代。他的推广实验是：给 Opus 5《指环王》第一段、100 万 token 的预算（约 10 美元），让它用 three.js 渲染出来。模型闷头干了约两小时，写了 5500 行代码，把故事程序化地渲染了出来。

真正值得琢磨的不是这个有点糙的结果，而是经济学：没有哪个正常人会花时间做这么定制化的东西，但 LLM 有无限的耐力和耐心。我们正在从「没人会做这个」走向「行啊，反正几乎免费」。他兴奋的是超定制化世界——按需生成的「X 版临时 GTA」，随时可以跳进去当旁观者或角色。

他还点出一个真实短板：LLM 很难审计自己的作品，因为它们没法原生地看视频、在游戏里游玩。Opus 5 只能慢慢截图检查渲染效果，还是做出一堆糙活——多模态、游戏内互动的原始能力依然缺位。

我们正在离开用「画一只骑自行车的鹈鹕 SVG」这种题来测 LLM 的阶段。作为推广这个思路的一个想法，我好奇：如果把《指环王》第一段给 Opus 5，给它 100 万 token 预算（约 10 美元），让它做一个 three.js 渲染会怎样。Opus 跑了约两小时，写了 5500 行代码，把故事（程序化地）渲染了出来。有点糙但好玩。不过想想也挺震撼：LLM 得在 (x,y,z) 坐标里摆放和编排各种多边形资产，还要写代码让它们动起来——而且它居然真能做出点东西。

我也喜欢这类例子，因为没有任何一个正常人会花时间写这么定制化的东西，但 LLM 有的是耐力和耐心。所以这是一个从「没人会做」到「行啊，反正几乎免费」的转变。类似的例子可能还有很多。我特别兴奋的是创造超定制化的世界——你可以想象把玩家丢进去，比如作为旁观 NPC 参与《指环王》的故事，或者当其中一个角色。有点像按需生成的临时版「X 的 GTA」。

最后一点：世界/游戏这个领域暴露了 LLM 的一个弱点——它们很难审计自己的工作，因为它们没法高效地原生感知视频或在游戏里游玩。这里 Opus 5 只能非常缓慢、痛苦地在不同位置截图，还搞砸了几次，做出一堆糙活。这是我认为仍然相当欠缺的原始能力（多模态、游戏内互动）的例子。

English

Andrej Karpathy, the OpenAI co-founder and former Tesla AI director, argues we're leaving the era of toy LLM tests like "create an SVG of a pelican on a bicycle." His generalization: he gave Opus 5 the first paragraph of The Lord of the Rings, a 1M-token budget (~$10), and asked for a three.js render. The model went off for about two hours and wrote 5,500 lines of code that procedurally rendered the story.

The deeper point isn't the janky result — it's the economics: no sane person would ever spend the time to build something this custom, but LLMs have all the stamina and patience in the world. We've moved from "no one would ever do this" to "sure, why not, it's ~free." He's excited about hyper-custom worlds — an ephemeral "GTA of X" you can drop into on demand.

He also flags a real weakness this exposes: LLMs can't easily audit their own work, because they can't natively watch videos or play games inside them. Opus 5 had to slowly take screenshots to check its render and still produced jank — raw multimodal, in-game capability is still missing.

I also like this kind of examples because no one in their right mind would ever spend the time to write something this custom but LLMs have all the stamina and patience in the world, so it's an example where we go from "no one would ever do this" to "sure, why not, it's ~free". There might be a lot more. But I'm excited about creating hyper custom worlds that you can imagine dropping players into, e.g. here to participate in the LoTR story as a spectator NPC, or one of the characters, or etc. Something like an ephemeral GTA of X on demand.

Last thought is that the domain of worlds/games exposes a weakness in LLMs: they can't easily audit their work because they aren't able to efficiently and natively perceive videos or play games within them. Here, Opus 5 had to very slowly and painstakingly take screenshots at different points, and it messed up a few times and created a bunch of jank. Deep Domains Go Vertical / 深度领域垂直化

消费级 AI 的能力已经够用，但数学、科学、法律、代码这些深度领域正要垂直起飞——而大多数人在日常生活中感受不到。

中文

Box CEO Aaron Levie 预判：AI 在个人生活和日常生产力里的表现，与它在数学、科学、法律、代码这些深度领域的能力，将出现越来越大的分化。

在某个阈值之前，能力提升在哪个领域都差不多，因为模型只是「普遍地稍微有用」。现在，深度领域的工作即将垂直起飞：大多数人不会直接感觉到，但这些领域的专家会。而且深度领域的能力需求没有天花板，不像消费场景那样需求相对容易满足。

他提醒会有「能力过剩」（capability overhang）——很多性能提升必须落到真实数据集和工作流上才能兑现。但突破生命科学、真实世界自动化、新型网络能力，正是这么来的。

我们将看到 AI 在个人生活和日常生产力中做的事，与它在数学、科学、法律、代码等深度领域能做的事，出现越来越大的分化。

在某个阈值之前，能力的提升在所有领域都被均匀感知到，因为模型只是普遍地变得略微有用。现在，深度领域的工作即将垂直起飞。

大多数人不会在日常中直接感受到这些好处（间接地，他们迟早会感受到），但这些领域的专家会。而且所需能力没有内在天花板——不像消费领域，需求相对直接就能被满足。

不过这常常会导致「能力过剩」：许多性能提升必须以应用的方式落到该领域的数据集和工作流上。但突破生命科学、真实世界自动化、新型网络能力等，归根结底就是这么来的。

English

Box CEO Aaron Levie predicts a growing divergence between what AI does in our personal lives and daily productivity versus what it can do in deep domains like math, science, legal, and coding.

Up to some threshold, capability gains felt even everywhere, because models were only mildly useful in general. Now deep-domain work is about to go vertical: most people won't directly notice, but the experts in these fields will. There's no inherent ceiling to needed capability — unlike consumer use cases, which get met relatively straightforwardly.

His caveat: expect a capability overhang — gains only materialize once applied to real datasets and workflows. But that's exactly how you get breakthroughs in life sciences, real-world automation, and new cyber capabilities.

No Priors Podcast

Netic 创始人兼 CEO Melisa Tokmak，前 Scale AI 高管（曾一手建立政府和大型企业部门），讲了她为什么押注「让世界运转」的民生服务业：HVAC、水管、屋顶、宠物护理这些十亿美元级的生意。Netic 站在公司和客户之间：agent 接电话、回短信、处理网站咨询，弄清真实需求，匹配运营规则，在正确的时间派出正确的技师。如今超过 70% 的客户是「AI first」——客户与公司的第一次接触，就是一个 Netic agent。

为什么不做 rollup：rollup 只为你买下的公司造产品，她想要的是一个能在所有真实生意上复利的产品。为什么不做机器人：那章还远在几十年后——建筑没有标准化、灵巧操作差得远，而且客户人生最糟的一天里，人情味依然重要。

她最锋利的一句留给大厂：「OpenAI 造产品快得惊人，杀产品也快得惊人。」这些行业的企业不想要这种节奏，它们要的是最后一公里：模型之上的 harness、编排和软件。十年前的问题是「Google 能搞定吗」，现在是「大厂能搞定吗」——答案一样：部分能，运营的脏活累活不能。

The takeaway: the most unsolved AI problem isn't better models — it's running mission-critical real-world businesses autonomously, and that demands being good at all three layers: models, harnesses, and orchestration.

Melisa Tokmak, founder and CEO of Netic and a former Scale AI executive who built its government and large-enterprise units, explains her bet on essential services: HVAC, plumbing, roofing, pet care — the billion-dollar businesses that keep the world running. Netic sits between these companies and their customers: agents handle inbound calls, texts, and web inquiries, figure out the real need, match it against the business's operational rules, and dispatch the right technician at the right time. Today over 70% of her customers are "AI first" — their customers' first interaction with the company is with a Netic agent.

On why not a rollup: rollups build products only for the companies you bought; she wants a product that compounds across every real-world business. On robotics: that chapter is decades away — buildings aren't standardized, dexterity is nowhere near enough, and the human element matters when it's the worst day of a customer's life.

Her sharpest line is about the labs: "OpenAI builds amazing products really fast, but it also kills them really fast." Enterprises in these industries don't want that cadence — they want the last mile: harnesses, orchestration, and software on top of the models. Ten years ago the question was "can Google do this?"; now it's "can the labs do this?" — and the answer is the same: some things yes, the operational grunt work no.

She also screens for one quality when hiring: sustained agency. "What's the hardest thing you've ever done?" — and she digs into whether people stuck with it, citing Martin Luther: the Christian shoemaker honors God not by putting crosses on the shoes, but by making the best shoes.
