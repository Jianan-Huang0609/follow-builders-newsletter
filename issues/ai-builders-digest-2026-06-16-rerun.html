Theme 01

Learning Loops as Company IP / 学习回路成为公司 IP

今天最重要的企业 AI 讨论不是模型排行榜，而是谁能把组织知识做成会复利的闭环。

中文

Swyx 抓住了 Satya Nadella 这次最关键的表达：loop 才是 IP。企业 AI 的真正机会不是选中“最强模型”，而是在模型之上搭一个 learning loop，让 human capital 和 token capital 一起复利。

这里最有用的变化是“所有权”。公司可以把任务外包给 AI，甚至把一部分 job 外包掉，但不能把自己的学习外包掉；真正长期有价值的是那个把重复工作、反馈和组织知识沉淀成下一次更好输出的回路。

这比“我们该买哪个模型”更接近企业 AI 的本质。模型可以替换，但 learning loop 里沉淀的是公司老员工式的判断力。

Satya 谈 loop 作为 IP：这是第一次我们能在人和数字系统之间创造真正的 cognitive loop，它会改变企业内部对工作的理解。

真正的机会不是挑最好的模型，而是在模型之上建立 learning loop，让 human capital 和 token capital 复利。你可以外包任务，甚至外包一个岗位，但你不能外包自己的学习。

优先级应该是建立 frontier ecosystem，而不只是 frontier model。每个组织都应该拥有编码自身机构知识的 learning loop，让人和 token capital 一起复利。

English

Swyx spotlights Satya Nadella's framing of loops as IP: the real enterprise opportunity is not picking the best model, but building a learning loop on top of models where human capital and token capital compound.

The useful shift here is ownership. A company can offload tasks, but it cannot offload learning; the durable asset is the loop that turns repeated work, feedback, and institutional knowledge into better future work.

This is a cleaner way to think about enterprise AI than 'which model should we buy?' The model is replaceable. The learning loop is where the company veteran knowledge lives.

Satya on loops as IP: https://t.co/87HBsQptdQ > This is the first time we can create a real cognitive loop between people and digital systems. That is a mind-bender, because it changes how we even conceptualize work inside an enterprise.

> This means the real opportunity is not in picking the best model but instead in building a learning loop on top of models where human capital and token capital compound. You can offload a task, or even a job, but you can never offload your learning

> In my view, our priority has to be building a frontier ecosystem, not just a frontier model, so value flows broadly across every company, every industry, and every country. One where every organization can own the learning loop that encodes its institutional knowledge, compounding its human and token capital.

AI Factory and the Intelligence Stack / AI 工厂与智能堆栈

Jensen Huang 把 agent、模型、能源、数据中心和应用层放进同一张产业图里：智能不是被检索出来的，而是被实时生产出来的。

TD
Training Data
Training Data
中文

核心 takeaway：NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang 把 AI 讲成一套新的工业系统：工厂把 electrons 变成 token，再把 token 变成 intelligence。

他最关键的区分很简单，但很有力量：旧计算是 retrieval-based，所以那些建筑叫 data centers；AI 计算是 generative，每次收到 context 和 query 都实时生成新的内容和决策。如果这个判断成立，世界需要的就不只是存数据的中心，而是生产智能的 generator。

Jensen 的“五层蛋糕”是他的投资地图：最底层是 energy，其上是 chips、computers、networking，再上是 land、power、data center operations 等 infrastructure，然后是 model 公司，最后是 application layer。他对个人的反常识提醒不是“AI 会抢你工作”，而是“你一定会输给会用 AI 的人”。

他也用 radiology 反驳岗位悲观论：AI 深度进入了放射科工作流，但 radiologist 需求反而上升，因为生产率提升扩大了医院能做的事。他真正想区分的是 task 和 purpose。AI 自动化 task，有价值的工作会围绕更高层的 purpose 重组。

计算产业用了 60 年 retrieval 模式，突然有一天变成实时生成。我们称之为 intelligence。

未来你看到的每个像素、听到的每段声音、看到的每个视频都会是原始生成的，而不是检索出来的。这意味着未来我们需要更多 generators。

我们把 electrons 放进 NVIDIA 这台机器，进到这个 factory，出来的是 numbers。这些 numbers 取决于怎么组合，可以变成语言、数学。

从产业角度看，可以把它想成一个五层蛋糕。

你不一定会因为 AI 失去工作，但你一定会因为某个会用 AI 的人而失去工作。

Task versus purpose。能理解吗？

English

The Takeaway: NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang frames AI as a new industrial system, where factories turn electrons into tokens and tokens into intelligence.

His core distinction is simple but powerful: old computing was retrieval-based, which is why we called the buildings data centers; AI computing is generative, producing original content and decisions every time it receives context and a query. If that is true, the world needs generators of intelligence, not just stores of data.

Jensen's five-layer cake is the investment map: energy at the bottom, then chips/computers/networking, then infrastructure like land and data center operations, then model companies, then the application layer. The contrarian warning is not 'AI will take your job,' but 'you will absolutely lose a job to someone who uses AI.'

He also pushes back on job fatalism with the radiology example: AI penetrated the workflow, but demand for radiologists went up because productivity expanded what hospitals could do. His bigger point is task versus purpose. AI automates tasks; valuable work reorganizes around higher-purpose jobs.

Went from a computer industry that was largely based on retrieval for sixty years and all of a sudden, one day, it's completely generated in real time. We call it intelligence.

every single pixel that you see, every single sound that you hear in the future, every video you see in the future will be originally generated, not retrieved. Which means in the future we need a lot more generators.

We then take the electrons into our machine called NVIDIA. Electrons now comes into our machine, comes in this factory, and what comes out are numbers. These numbers, depending on how you combine them, turns into language, math.

The way to think about the industrial view is think of it as a five layer cake.

You may or may not lose a job to an AI. But you will absolutely lose a job to someone who uses AI.

Task versus purpose. Does that make sense?

Control, Portability, and Open Weights / 控制权、可迁移性与开放权重

当模型可能受政策、平台和供应链影响时，builder 们自然会重新讨论开放权重、开源和长期控制权。

中文

Aaron Levie 判断，最近模型可用性的不确定性会让 open weights models 成为大赢家。他的重点既是技术问题，也是地缘问题：如果某个模型随时可能对一个国家的用户或企业不可用，那依赖某个国家的技术栈就会变成真实商业风险。

下游激励很明显：主要国家会更想走 sovereign AI 路线，而最现实的方案大概率就是 open weights。Levie 给美国的提醒是，如果监管主要压在 model layer，而不是 applied layer，长期可能会削弱美国在这个技术栈里的领导地位。

这一切的大赢家会是 open weights models。两天前还只是理论上的风险——模型可能被撤回——现在有了新先例，这对整个领域是巨大胜利。

如果一个模型随时可能对你的国家用户或企业不可用，那么依赖某个特定国家的技术就会带来真实风险。

结果是主要国家会被迫走自己的 AI 发展路线，长期削弱美国在这个技术栈里的领导地位。其他国家最可能依赖的解决方案是 open weights models，而这些目前通常不是来自美国。

美国在决定如何、在 stack 的哪里监管 AI 时，应该考虑这些下游影响。同时我们应该做更多 OSS innovation。

English

Aaron Levie argues that recent model-availability uncertainty is a major win for open weights models. His point is geopolitical as much as technical: if a model can become unavailable to a country's users or businesses at any moment, relying on one country's technology stack becomes a real business risk.

The downstream incentive is obvious: major countries will push harder toward sovereign AI, and the most likely practical solution is open weights. Levie's warning to the US is that regulating at the model layer rather than the applied layer can accidentally weaken America's leadership over time.

The big winner in all of this is going to be open weights models. This is a huge win for the field, as a risk that was entirely theoretical and untested 2 days ago (that a model could be pulled back), now has a new precedent that’s been set.

If at any moment a model can be become unavailable to your country’s users or businesses, this poses very real risk on relying on technology from a particular country.

As a result, it forces major countries to charter their own path on AI development, which reduces America’s leadership role in this tech stack over time. The most likely solution that other countries will rely on is open weights models, which currently is generally not coming from the US.

America should be considering all of these downstream implications as it decides how and where in the stack to be regulating AI. At the same time, we should be doing a ton more OSS innovation.

Skills Are Bottled Practice / Skill 是被装瓶的实践

真正有用的 skill 不是一开始写出来的说明书，而是做过、修过、踩过坑之后沉淀下来的操作经验。

中文

Zara Zhang 给出了今天最接地气的 skill 建议：你不是靠“写一个 skill”来做出好 skill。你是先把事情做了，修 20 次，然后让 AI 把你刚刚学到的一切装瓶。

她的补充更短：“You make a skill by ending with one, not starting with one.” 这是对当前 skill 热的一次很有用的纠偏。skill 这个 artifact 应该出现在实践之后，而不是实践之前。

你不是靠写一个 skill 来做出好 skill。你是靠先做那件事，修 20 次，然后告诉 AI 把你刚做过的一切装瓶。

你是以一个 skill 结束，而不是以一个 skill 开始。

English

Zara Zhang gives the most grounded skill-building advice of the day: you do not make a good skill by writing a skill. You make it by doing the thing, fixing it twenty times, and then bottling up what you learned.

Her follow-up is even shorter: 'You make a skill by ending with one, not starting with one.' That is a useful correction to the current skill hype. The artifact should come after practice, not before it.

You don't make a good skill by writing a skill. You make it by doing the thing, fixing it 20 times, then telling the AI to bottle up everything you just did.
