Software Economics, Revisited / 重访软件经济学

消费者到底怎么花钱？这个老问题对 AI 产品的定价和分发有直接影响。

AM
Amjad Masad
@amasad
中文

Replit CEO Amjad Masad 指出了一个决定整个软件市场格局的结构性事实：「消费者的钱花在食物、房租、娱乐、通讯和购物上。软件通常是公司买单。所以除了 Netflix、Spotify 这些，你很难想到真正*巨大的*消费者订阅业务。」

这对 AI 产品有直接影响。大多数消费者 AI 工具都是让个人自掏腰包的订阅制。如果 Masad 的观察成立——消费者根本上就没有给软件留预算——那消费者 AI 公司面临支付意愿的天然天花板，除非它们要么变成娱乐（像 Netflix/Spotify），要么变成不可替代的基础设施（像通讯），要么找到让雇主买单的路径。企业 AI 的淘金热可能不只是因为利润率高——还可能是因为只有企业预算那一行里才有软件的钱。

消费者的钱花在食物、房租、娱乐、通讯和购物上。软件通常由公司买单。所以除了 Netflix、Spotify，很难想到真正巨大的消费者订阅业务。

English

Replit CEO Amjad Masad points out a structural fact about consumer spending that shapes the entire software market: 'Consumers spend money on food, rent, and entertainment, phone & internet, and shopping. Software is typically bought by their companies. Which is why outside of Netflix, Spotify, etc it's hard to think of *huge* consumer subscription businesses.'

This has direct implications for AI products. Most consumer AI tools are subscription businesses that ask individuals to pay out of pocket. If Masad's observation holds—that consumers fundamentally don't budget for software—then consumer AI companies face an uphill battle on willingness-to-pay, unless they either become entertainment (like Netflix/Spotify), become indispensable utilities (like phone/internet), or find a way to get employers to foot the bill. The enterprise AI gold rush might not be just about higher margins—it might be about tapping the only budget line that actually exists for software.

Content Strategy in the AI Era / AI 时代的内容策略

当 AI 能做内容时，人类创作者的价值锚点反而更清晰了。

中文

Zara Zhang 的内容策略框架比大多数付费课程都实用：「总有人问我'刚开始做社交媒体内容，该聊什么？'我的答案是：朋友/同事/熟人最常问你的三个问题是什么？把那些回答做成视频或帖子。就用你平时跟他们说话的方式讲出来。那就是你的定位。」

她还加了一条实操规则：「一件事你重复讲过三次以上，就值得做成视频。」以及一个私人动机：「我做社交媒体的初衷之一，就是厌倦了在现实生活中不断重复自己。」201 个赞说明这话引起了共鸣。这个框架在 AI 时代只会更相关——当谁都能批量生成泛内容时，优势属于那些通过亲身经历赢得回答特定问题资格的人。

总有人问我刚开始做社交媒体内容该聊什么。我的答案：朋友同事最常问你的三个问题是什么？把回答做成视频帖子，用平常说话的方式讲。那就是你的定位。一件事重复讲过三次以上，就值得做成视频。我做社交媒体的初衷就是厌倦了在现实中不断重复自己。

English

Zara Zhang has a content strategy framework that's better than most paid courses: 'Whenever someone asks me "I'm just getting started with posting content on social media; what should I talk about?" My answer is: What are the top 3 questions that you get asked most frequently by friends/acquaintances/colleagues? Turn those answers into videos/posts. Just say it like how you said it to them in real life. That is your positioning.'

She adds a practical rule: 'If you have repeatedly said something for more than 3 times, it's worth turning into a video.' And a personal motivation: 'One of my initial motivations for posting on social media was because I was tired of repeating myself in real life.' At 201 likes, this resonated—and it's a framework that becomes more relevant, not less, in an AI era. When anyone can generate generic content, the advantage goes to people who have earned the right to answer specific questions through lived experience.
