The Chip War: Why Speed Is Everything / 芯片战争：推理速度决定一切

Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman 在这期播客里把芯片行业的底层逻辑讲得比任何分析师都清楚：不是算力，不是模型，是推理速度在重塑整个半导体格局。

AF
Andrew Feldman
CEO, Cerebras
中文

Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman 把芯片战争的核心讲透了：唯一的度量是每用户每秒 token 数。他的 Netflix 类比一刀见血——网速慢的时候，Netflix 寄 DVD；网速快了，它变成了电影公司。速度不只是加速，而是改变可能性本身。「慢速搜索的市场有多大？拨号上网的市场有多大？零。」AI 推理同理。

他说出了一个大多数芯片评论没讲到的事实：GPU 生态被三个隐藏瓶颈卡死了。HBM 内存——全球三家供应商，全部售罄。台积电 CoWoS 先进封装——同样售罄。3nm 产能——最拥挤的制程节点。Cerebras 靠架构选择绕开了全部三个：SRAM 替代 HBM、晶圆级集成替代 CoWoS、5nm 替代 3nm。不是运气，是十年前就押下的赌注。

关于 NVIDIA 护城河，他的判断很直接：「推理没有护城河。从 GPU 切到我们，只需要八次按键。」数据站在他这边：两年内，NVIDIA 在训练最先进模型上丢了 70% 份额。Gemini 用 TPU 训练，Claude 用 Trainium，OpenAI 也在跟进。CUDA 叙事和数据正在反向运动。

创始人故事：2020 年，Cerebras 用一整片 300mm 晶圆做了一颗 46000 平方毫米的芯片——此前纪录是 840。「没人关心。没人买。所有人都说我们疯了。」他们的策略是有意为之：「你永远不可能靠做得'好一点点'来打败 NVIDIA。你必须好 10 倍、20 倍、50 倍。」十年沙漠，换来史上最大半导体 IPO、超过 200 亿美元的 OpenAI 订单，以及地球上最快的推理硬件。

这是计算机工业史上最大的芯片，比 GPU 大 58 倍。对 AI 来说，更大的芯片更快地处理信息，你能更快拿到答案。

慢速搜索的市场有多大？拨号上网的市场有多大？零。

推理没有护城河。从 GPU 切到我们，只需要八次按键。

我们解决了计算史上没人解决过的问题，2020 年出货，没人关心。没人关心，没人买，没人关心。

你永远不可能靠比 NVIDIA 好一点点来打败它。正确的策略是去攻克那些极其困难的工程问题，好 10 倍、20 倍、50 倍。

English

Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman explains why the AI chip industry has reorganized around one metric: tokens per second per user. His analogy is sharp — when the internet was slow, Netflix mailed DVDs; when it got fast, they became a movie studio. Speed doesn't just accelerate, it transforms what's possible. The same holds for AI inference: 'How big is the market for slow search? How big is the market for dial-up? It's zero.'

The insight that separates him from most chip commentary: three hidden bottlenecks are strangling the GPU ecosystem. HBM memory — three suppliers worldwide, all sold out. TSMC's CoWoS advanced packaging — also sold out. 3nm fab space — the most congested manufacturing node. Cerebras dodged all three by architectural choice: SRAM instead of HBM, wafer-scale integration instead of CoWoS, and 5nm instead of 3nm. This isn't luck — it's a decade of deliberate bets.

On NVIDIA's moat: 'There's no moat in inference. It takes you eight keystrokes to move from a GPU to us in the cloud.' The data backs him up: in two years, NVIDIA lost 70% share of training state-of-the-art models. Gemini trains on TPUs, Claude on Trainium, and OpenAI is following. The CUDA narrative and the data are moving in opposite directions.

The founder story: Cerebras solved wafer-scale computing in 2020 — a single 46,000mm² chip from a 300mm wafer, vs the previous record of 840mm². 'Nobody cared. Nobody bought it. Everybody said we were crazy.' Their strategy was deliberate: 'You will never beat NVIDIA by building something a little bit better. You have to build something 10x, 20x, 50x better.' A decade in the desert later: the largest semiconductor IPO in history, a $20B+ OpenAI deal, and the fastest inference hardware on the planet.

This is the largest chip built in the history of the computer industry. It's 58 times larger than a GPU. And for AI, bigger chips process information more quickly, and therefore, you get answers in less time.

How big is the market for slow search? How big is the market for dial up? It's zero.

There's no moat in inference. It takes you eight keystrokes to move from a GPU to us in the cloud.

We solved a problem that nobody in the history of computing solved, and we delivered it in 2020, and nobody cared. Nobody cared. Nobody bought it. Nobody cared.

You will never beat a great company like NVIDIA by doing something a little bit better. The right strategy would be to do something incredibly hard in engineering that was way better, ten, fifteen, twenty, thirty, fifty times better.

English

Meta's AI Director Madhu Guru surfaces a security problem most engineering teams haven't started thinking about: how do you manage security for effectively infinite agents, when identity and access management was designed for a finite number of human employees?

Builder Judgment / 构建者的判断力

工具越强，判断力越值钱。Levie 和 Masad 从不同角度在说同一件事：AI 是放大器，它放大的是你本来就有的东西。

中文

Box CEO Aaron Levie 给出了一个理解 AI 经济影响的清晰框架：AI 是你已经掌握的领域的放大器。懂行的工程师用 agent 能做更多事，正是因为他知道 agent 跑偏了该怎么纠正。有审美的设计师用 AI 出活，质量远超没有设计眼光的人。

第三种人——没有判断力、也不想培养判断力——只会产出垃圾，不会带来真正的经济产出。他的反共识结论：AI 工具越强，专业化越重要，而不是越不重要。因为市场对质量的期望随着工具能力一起上涨。把手艺练好不是过时了——是有效使用 AI 的前提条件。

理解 AI 最好的方式是把它看作你已经了解的领域的放大器。第三种情况——没有判断力、也不想培养——基本只会产出垃圾，不会产生真正的经济价值。

因此，专业化会继续重要，甚至随着工具越来越强而更加重要。因为市场的期望变高了。把任何一门手艺做好，在未来依然是必需的。

English

Box CEO Aaron Levie delivers a sharp framework for thinking about AI's economic impact: AI is a force multiplier for the fields you already understand. The expert engineer with agents will do far more productive work precisely because they know how to steer the agent when it goes off course. The designer with AI will produce far better outcomes than someone without an eye for design.

The third category — no existing judgment, no interest in developing it — will produce slop and won't drive economically productive activity. His contrarian conclusion: specialization will become more important, not less, as AI tools get more powerful. Because the market's expectation of quality keeps rising alongside what the tools can do. Getting good at a craft isn't obsolete — it's the prerequisite for wielding AI effectively.

The best way to think about AI is as a force multiplier for the fields you already know about (or for the rate at which you want to learn about a new one). The 3rd category - no existing judgment, no interest in developing it - will basically produce slop and won't drive that much economically productive activity.

As a result of this dynamic, specialization will continue to be important, if not even more so as the tools get more powerful. Because the expectation of the market gets to be much higher. Getting good at any craft will continue to be necessary in the future.
