Theme 01

From Model Capability to Real Work / 从能力到工作流

真正的 AI 影响不会只体现在 benchmark 上，而要经过数据准备、流程重构和组织变更，才能抵达用户。

中文

Box CEO Aaron Levie 认为，AI 能力的进步会远快于它在经济中的扩散。企业还得准备数据、重做流程、推动变革、对齐新的工作方式，现实世界也有自己的速度。这里恰恰藏着机会：把 superintelligence 接到真实运营环节的团队，可能比只追求模型能力的团队更有价值。

AI 能力与 GDP 影响之间的落差，原因是 AI 的扩散会比人们想象的更慢，而且短期内很多影响很难直接体现在 GDP 里。即使把世界上最强的 superintelligence 放进工作流，企业仍会受制于‘企业物理学’：准备数据、接入 pipeline、重做流程、推动变革、对齐新的工作方式。 解决这些之后，还要受现实世界的速度限制：等待客户回复 proposal、等待项目许可、药物研发多年后才抵达消费者。未来十年会是 AI 扩散的十年，真正的机会在于搭建 superintelligence 与现实工作流之间的桥梁。

English

Box CEO Aaron Levie argues that AI diffusion will take longer than capability progress suggests. The bottleneck is not intelligence alone: companies still have to prepare data, redesign processes, align on new workflows, and wait for the physical world to move. That gap is also the opportunity: builders who connect superintelligence to real operations may create more value than builders who only improve the model.

The way to the reconcile capability level of AI vs. GDP impact is that the diffusion of AI will take much longer than people think. And it will also show up in ways that are hard to measure in GDP immediately. You could bring the world’s greatest superintelligence to many workflows, and still be bound by the laws of corporate physics: getting data prepared and put into a pipeline, process reengineering and change management, aligning on how the new workflow should function, and so on. Even after you solve all that, you’re still bound by the speed of the real world: waiting for a customer to respond to a proposal, getting a permit for a project, a drug discovery pipeline taking years to eventually reach the consumer, and so on. Not to mention lots of positive daily AI use-cases are entirely net neutral to GDP, at least in the near term. AI diffusion is going to be the theme of the next decade. The upside is that there’s a tremendous amount of opportunity in building the bridges between superintelligence and real-world workflows.

In short, by lowering the cost of code, we are going to do so much more with it. This will result in software becoming more useful and the leverage of engineers going up dramatically, which coincidentally will mean we need more of them, not fewer.

中文

Dan Shipper 质疑了一个常见前提：automation 不一定只是减少人的工作。把 job 拆成 tasks 是有用的分析方法，但工具改变了人们注意什么、在乎什么，也会不断创造新的任务。要认真讨论 AI 对就业的影响，就不能只计算消失的劳动，还要计算被创造出来的工作。

这是一份设计得很好、质量很高的报告，但有两个重要假设会显著改变分析：一是它假设 jobs 可以拆解成 tasks；二是它把 automation 创造的人类任务建模为 automated tasks 的一部分，也就是默认 automation 总会减少人力。 但 automation 总会减少 human work 吗？以我的经验，不会。automation 经常会给人类创造出成倍的新工作。如果认真对待这一点，我们对 automation 和 jobs 的看法应该会发生很大变化。

English

Dan Shipper challenges the assumption that automation simply removes human work. Tasks are a useful way to analyze jobs, but tools also create new tasks by changing what people notice, care about, and consider good work. Any serious account of AI's economic impact therefore needs to model work creation, not only the labor that disappears.

this is a very good and well designed report however, two impt assumptions in it that significantly change the analysis once you start to play with it: 1. the report assumes that jobs can be decomposed into tasks 2. the report models human tasks created by automation always as a fraction of automated tasks. in other words, the underlying assumptions is automation always reduces human labor (or at best leaves it the same) taken in reverse order: does automation always reduce human work? in my experience, the answer is no. automation often creates multiples more work for humans. ... but if you take it seriously, it shoudl substantially change your perspective automation and jobs

English

Peter Yang shares a simple stress test for a model's thinking: ask for brutally honest feedback on the blind spots holding you back, using the full context of the prompt. He says Astra's response was especially blunt. The useful idea is to test models on uncomfortable judgment, not only on polished benchmark questions.
