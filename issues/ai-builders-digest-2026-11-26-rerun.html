The New Work Interface / 新的工作接口

coding 可能只是知识工作的试验田：人先协调 agent，最后只给它目标。

中文

Aaron Levie 把 coding 看成大量知识工作的未来窗口。第一步，人们从亲自做 research、打字和创造价值，转向协调 agent；再往后，连协调本身都可能拖慢 agent，于是人只需要给 agent 一个要优化的目标。

这个模式会扩展到制造、建筑、生命科学、材料科学和 cyber。真正的限制是验证：工作越难检查，人就越需要留在 loop 里。

大多数工作的第一步，是从手工完成 research、打字和价值创造，转向协调 agent 的工作。

我们会给 agent 要优化的目标，让它们去解决越来越大的问题。

一个大障碍是，这些工作很多仍然无法验证。

English

Aaron Levie sees coding as a window into the future of a large share of knowledge work. First, people move from doing the research, typing, and value creation themselves to coordinating agents. Then even that coordination can become a bottleneck, and humans start giving agents goals to optimize against.

The pattern will reach manufacturing, architecture, life sciences, materials, and cyber. The hard limit is verification: wherever work cannot be checked easily, humans stay in the loop longer.

The first step for most jobs is that we go from doing most of the research, typing, and value creation manually ... to soon coordinating the work that agents are doing.

We will be giving agents goals to optimize against and they’ll go off on and increasingly solve larger problems for us.

One big hurdle ... will be that much of this work remains non-verifiable.

Theme 04

Beyond Rationality / 超越理性模型

从 poker 到 diplomacy 再到 reasoning，真正难的不是算出最优解，而是理解真实的人会怎样行动。

TD
Training Data
Training Data
中文

OpenAI 的 Noam Brown 回顾了自己从 poker bot 到 diplomacy、再到 reasoning 的路径。最反直觉的教训是：game theory 那个干净的前提——所有人都是理性的——一旦面对真实的人就会失效。最后通牒博弈里，理论上合理的微小报价会因为不公平感而被拒绝；按同一逻辑构建的 diplomacy bot 也会犯类似错误。

更好的路线是用人类数据训练模型，尽可能模仿人的行为，再根据人真实的行为方式判断下一步。Brown 最值得记住的一句是：「不要假设理性，而要假设人会像人一样行动。」

game theory 的问题在于，它假设其他玩家都是理性的，但人其实并不理性。

要做出超人的 diplomacy AI，真正的方法是用人类数据训练，去理解人。

不要假设理性，而要假设人会像人一样行动。

English

OpenAI's Noam Brown traces his path from poker bots to diplomacy and reasoning. The surprising lesson is that game theory's clean assumption—that everyone is rational—breaks down when you need to negotiate with actual people. A perfectly rational offer in the ultimatum game can be rejected because it feels unfair; diplomacy bots built around the same logic made similar mistakes.

The better route was to train on human data, imitate people closely, and then reason about the best action given how people actually behave. Brown's memorable summary: 'Do not assume rationality, but to just assume that people are gonna behave like people.'

The problem with game theory is that it assumes ... that all the other players are rational, and people are not actually rational.

The real way to make a superhuman diplomacy AI is to train on human data, to just understand people.

Do not assume rationality, but to just assume that people are gonna behave like people.
