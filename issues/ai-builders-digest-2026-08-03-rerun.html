Theme 01

The Company Agent / 公司级 Agent 时代

这周最重磅的信号：agent 不再只是帮你写代码的工具，开始成为公司内部运营的总入口、以及替你去吵架的外交官。

中文

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch 揭开了自家公司内部运营 agent 的面纱，它叫 @v：现在 Vercel 的每一项日常工作都会经过它，交互量和 token 消耗都在指数级增长。它管财务、沟通、文档、营销、工程和商业分析，按用户维护记忆和日程，连 skills.sh 破 100 万 skill 这种消息，都是 Rauch 让它定期检查后提醒自己的。

他的预测很直接：「从这里可以外推，总有一天 AI agent 会运营整个公司。」但更有信息量的是他的补充设计笔记：Vercel 的 agent 一度多到「像每个 agent 都有自己的域名」，体验很糟。解法是把 @v 同时做成 agent 和路由器，像一个 monolith，需要时通过网络把活转包给别的 agent。「拿不准的时候，就 @v。」

底层主张：如果 agent 会成为现代公司的基础设施，那你就该掌控从源码、运行时、数据到 token 的整条链路。「那不是你的 agent，那是他们的 agent。」

我们建了一个驱动公司内部运营的 agent，叫 @v。现在 Vercel 的每一项日常工作都会经过它，交互量和 token 使用都在指数增长。 从这里可以外推，总有一天 AI agent 会运营整个公司。它是财务、沟通、文档、营销、工程、商业分析方面的专家，由我们给它的 skill 初始化并持续更新，但它自己也在不断改进。 它已经按用户维护记忆、个性化工作流和日程。比如我知道 skills.sh 达到 100 万 skill，是因为我让 @v 定期检查并提醒我。 它由 @evedev_ 驱动，也是 evedev 设计的基础。我们做出来了，效果非常好，我们认为每家公司都应该有一个。 我知道你也可以选一个现成的 BigAI slack 集成。但那是他们的 agent，不是你的 agent。如果 agent 成为现代公司的基础（甚至同义词），那么从源码、运行时、数据到 token 都由你完全掌控，这件事的分量相当大。

补充：我们其实早就有一堆 agent 了，准确说是太多了。Vercel 的团队和个人各自构建部署了几十个 agent。 用网络的类比，那就像每个 agent 都有自己的域名，这不是好的体验。我只想要 ${company}.com！ @v 帮我们解决了这个问题。它既是 agent 又是路由器，有子 agent、skills，知道怎么把工作委派给其他 agent。 另一个类比：它有点像 monolith 或 monorepo，但当你需要时，有通过网络把任务 proxy 给其他 agent 的逃生通道。这和互联网路由的规模化演进没什么两样。 当然也会有人直接使用的专用 agent，类比是 subdomain.company.com。有些 agent 值得有自己的「前门」，但我不认为应该有很多。 拿不准的时候，就 @v。

Theme 02

The Automation Order / 自动化的先后顺序

Levie 说最先被自动化的是最「可验证」的硬工作；Dan Shipper 则写下被取代者要经历的心理三阶段。两篇合起来，是今天最完整的图景。

中文

Box CEO Aaron Levie 给出了一个反直觉的分析框架：世界上最「难」的几类工作，数学、网络安全、写代码，反而会最先被自动化，原因恰恰是可验证。输出能客观检验，意味着训练模型时 reward 信号更清晰，模型跑起来之后也能规模化地验证结果。

反面是：客户会接受哪些法律条款、该跑什么营销战役、销售对象想听什么话、业务该定什么财务目标，这些都没有标准答案。它们依赖上下文、依赖决策者的判断和风险偏好，结果往往要等很久才能验证。

他的结论：即使模型能力继续指数级上升，应用层依然有大量活要干，甚至很多流程本身要跟着变，才能吃满自动化的红利。我们可能还需要发明全新的能力，像当年测试软件那样去「测试」知识工作。

我们将面对一个奇怪的动态：世界上一些「最难」的工作，反而会最先被自动化，主要就是因为它们的可验证性。 数学、网络安全和代码，虽然都是极难且高价值的领域，但有一个好处：可以客观检验它们是否正确。这带来两个直接好处：训练模型时 reward 信号更清晰；模型运行后，你可以规模化地测试结果，确认它是否正常工作。 相反，其他工作领域几乎没有即时可验证性。客户会接受哪些法律条款、根据变化的情绪该跑什么营销战役、销售对象想听什么话、公司该定什么财务目标和预算，诸如此类。 这些领域有各种变化的内外因素，没有「唯一正确答案」，依赖操作者的判断和风险偏好，对先拿到正确输入上下文极其敏感，而且很多时候，模型给出结果后很久都无法知道正确答案。 这个区分的含义是：即使模型能力继续指数级提升，应用层依然有大量事情要做，而不仅仅是模型本身；很多流程本身也需要随时间改变，才能从自动化中拿到全部收益。我们甚至可能需要全新的能力，像当年拥有软件测试那样，去「测试」知识工作。

English

Box CEO Aaron Levie lays out a contrarian framework: some of the 'hardest' work in the world, math, cyber and code, is actually most prone to automation first, precisely because it's verifiable. Objectively testable output means clearer reward signals when training models, and scalable testing of results once they run.

The flip side: which legal clauses your client will accept, which marketing campaign to run, which message a sales prospect wants to hear, what financial targets to set, all have no single right answer. They depend on context, operator risk appetite, and can't be judged for months.

His conclusion: even as model capability keeps rising exponentially, a large applied layer will remain, and we may need to invent whole new capabilities to 'test' knowledge work the way we've tested software.

We’re going to be in for a strange dynamic which is that some of the “hardest” work in the world is actually prone to automation first, particularly due to its verifiability. Math, cyber, and code -while being insanely hard and high value fields- have the benefit of being able to be tested that it’s correct objectively. This has two immediate benefits: the training of the models offers clearer reward signals, and then the running of the models allows you to know that it’s working properly because you can test the results in a scalable way. Conversely, in other domains of work, there’s much less instant verifiability. Which legal clauses your client will agree to, what marketing campaign to run with based on changing sentiment, which message your sales prospect will want to hear, what financial targets and budget to set for a business, and so on. All of these domains have changing internal and external factors, they don’t have “one right answer”, they rely on the opinions and risk levels of the operators, they’re highly sensitive to getting the right input context first, and in many cases the right answer can’t even be known for quite some time after the model generates the results. The implications of this distinction are that -even as model capability continues to increase exponentially- there will be a lot done at the applied AI layer than just the the model itself, and much of the processes themselves will even need to change over time to get the full gains from automation. We may even need all new capabilities to be able to “test” knowledge work over time as we have had with software.

中文

Every 的 CEO Dan Shipper 写了一篇关于「被 AI 取代的感觉」的文章：当模型接管一件曾经每一步都需要你的任务时，那是一次巨大的 agency rupture（能动性断裂）——「你不再被需要了，而且你毫无发言权。这是一种死亡。」

他总结了可预测的三阶段：第一阶段你只看见 AI（「AI 解决了某某问题」）；第二阶段你看见人类脚手架（「我的工作就是给 Claude 当保姆」）；第三阶段你重建能动性，改口说「这事是我做的」，AI 变得隐形，成了帮你快进无聊部分的工具。

他的判断：能否把这种断裂消化成好奇心和玩心，是判断一个人会不会在新 AI 经济里如鱼得水的领先指标。他还补了一条哲学推论：如果「应当蕴含能够」（ought implies can），而技术重塑了人类能力的边界，那么技术也在重塑我们的道德直觉。

当你看到一个语言模型在做一件曾经每一步都需要你的任务、而现在不需要了，那是一次巨大的能动性断裂。 我们习惯把自己等同于任务和产出，这种断裂近似于失去身份和自我感。 你不再被需要了，而且你毫无发言权。这是一种死亡。 不过，AI 带来的能动性断裂似乎遵循一个可预测的模式： - 最初的断裂。你只看见 AI 在做你以前做的事。在你的感知里 AI 被高亮，而围绕它的人类工作完全隐形。看我们的语言就知道了：「AI 解决了某某 Erdős 问题」，其实就是说我们正处于最初的断裂阶段。 - 看见人类脚手架。和模型相处一段时间后，我们开始看到模型的边界，开始看见你和另一端的人类为了让模型做好工作所需的脚手架。这时候脚手架感觉像是次要工作，但它确实是工作。我们开始说：「是 AI 在提示我」，或者「我的工作就是给 Claude 当保姆」。 - 能动性重建。和当前能力水平的模型相处足够久之后，我们重建一种以自己为核心的能动性，模型的贡献基本隐形。它现在是工具，帮你快进无聊或重复的部分，曾经看起来像脚手架的东西，现在看起来才是真正的工作本身，也就是有趣的部分。（随之而来的是你对「高质量工作」的定义变了，地板抬高了，天花板也抬高了。） 判断你是否走到这一步：你不再说「这是 AI 做的」，而是说「这是我做的」，用没用 AI 是不言自明的。比如我不会去问 @every 的人，产品最新功能是不是 AI 做的。当然是！但我们太习惯这种能力了，它变得隐形，我们重新把自己放回中心。 我的理论是：能否把能动性断裂消化成玩心和好奇心，是判断一个人是否喜欢并擅长新 AI 经济的领先指标。 每次能力跃迁，都会给已被 AI 触及的领域带来新的断裂，也会给直到最近还未受影响的群体（比如数学家）带来新的断裂。 如果你知道这个周期长什么样，就更容易在不崩溃的前提下骑上它。我觉得 @every 的多数人天生就擅长这个。

如果「应当蕴含能够」，而技术重塑了人类能力的版图，那么技术就重塑了「应当」。 我们应该预期，AI 在改变我们能力的同时，也会从根本上改变我们的道德直觉。

English

Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, writes about what it feels like when a model takes over a task that used to require you at every step: 'a gigantic agency rupture... you are no longer required, and you had no say in the matter. It's a kind of death.'

His three-phase pattern: first you see only the AI ('AI solved the problem'); then you see the human scaffolding around it ('my job is just to babysit Claude'); finally you reconstruct agency and say 'I did this' while the AI becomes invisible, a tool that fast-forwards the boring parts.

His theory: the ability to metabolize these ruptures into playfulness and curiosity is the leading indicator for who thrives in the new AI economy. He adds a philosophical coda: if ought implies can and technology reshapes human abilities, then technology reshapes our moral intuitions too.

when you experience a language model doing a task that used to require you at every step and now doesn't, it's a gigantic agency rupture. we tend to equate ourselves with our tasks, our outputs, and a rupture of this kind is akin to the loss of your identity and sense of self. you are no longer required, and you had no say in the matter. it's a kind of death. however, agency ruptures with AI seem to follow a predictable pattern: - Initial agency rupture. Here you see the AI doing what you used to do, and you see only the AI. In your perception of reality the AI is highlighted, and the human work around getting the AI to do the thing, is invisible. you can see this in the way we use language: "AI solved XYZ Erdos Problem" is another way to say we're in the initial rupture phase. - Seeing human scaffolding. After a little bit of experience with the model and its new capibility—programming, writing, math, etc—we start to see the edges of what's possible with the model. Here, instead of just seeing the AI doing the task, we start to see the scaffolding required by you and other humans on either end to make sure the model does its work. At this stage, the scaffolding can feel like a secondary part of the work, but it is work nonetheless. We begin to say things like, "The AI is prompting me", or "My job is just to babysit Claude." - Agency reconstruction. After a long enough time with the model at its current level of capability, we start to reconstruct a new sense of agency that centers our own role in the work being done, and makes the model's contribution mostly invisible. It is now a tool, it fast-forwards you through the boring or repetitive parts of the work, and what used to seem like scaffolding now seems like the actual work itself—the interesting part. You've had enough experience with the scaffolding to see its nuances, and how hard it is to get the model to reliably output high quality work. (As part of this, your conception of what quality work is changes—the floor is raised, and so is the ceiling.) You can tell you're at this point because you stop saying "The AI did this" and just say, "I did this" and the fact that you used AI is implied. For example, it would be weird for me to talk to someone @every and ask them if the AI built the most recent feature of a product. Of course it did! But we're so used to the capability that it becomes invisible, and we re-center ourselves and our own conception of agency. My theory is that the ability to metabolize agency ruptures and turn them into playfulness, and curiosity is a good leading indicator for whether you're a person who likes and excels in the new AI economy or not. Each time capabilities jump, you get new ruptures for existing fields touched by AI. And you get new ruptures for naive populations (like mathemeticians) who until recently hadn't been affected. if you know what the cycle looks like, it makes it much easier to ride it without freaking out. i think most people @every are naturally good at this

The Frontier Lab / 寻找 Transformer 继任者

两位站在前沿实验室核心位置的研究者出来创业，第一站就是换掉 Transformer，并且把实验室本身做成自动化机器。

TD
Training Data
Training Data Podcast
中文

Jerry Tworek（前 OpenAI VP，主管过 Strawberry 和 reasoning 团队）和 Rohan Anil（Gemini 四大预训练负责人之一，出身 Google Brain，后来去了 Anthropic）共同创办了 Core Automation，旧金山当下最热的 neo lab 之一。他们的出发点刻意反共识：「替换 Transformer 的第一步，是深切体会它把我们带到了多远。」规模化预训练和 RL 都已经被「掌握」，瓶颈现在是架构本身。

更深的论证是：模型在实验室里训练、在真实世界里部署，而 benchmark 已经和训练数据同分布，真实任务却混乱得多。Jerry 说他 2024 年曾预测 AGI 在 2025 年到来，但规模化 RL 并没有解决真实世界任务。他的答案是让模型在 test time 学习、在用户数据上学习：in-context learning 太浅（用 Codex 大概 20 分钟就得压缩上下文），fine-tuning 逃不过灾难性遗忘，所以需要一个能在架构层表达「学习本身」的新算法。

实验室的运作方式本身也是一个赌注：做「全世界最自动化的实验室」，靠一个小而 agent 密集的团队跑出大实验室跑不动的实验循环。Rohan 的六个月路线图核心是自动化 kernel 生成：他们和 GPU Mode 合办的 QR kernel 竞赛找到了比 cuSOLVER 快 60 倍的 kernel，但那是全球大约三个人、花 10 万美元 coding agent 额度才做到的。Jerry 的 AGI 标准也很严格：「一个能在没有任何人类介入的情况下自我改进的模型」。

替换 Transformer 的第一步，是深切体会它把我们带到了多远。

我们已经非常擅长训练超大规模模型，掌握了两套算法：大规模预训练和大规模强化学习。我经常问自己，机器学习的下一个节点是什么。我认为此刻，通往更好模型和更智能系统的瓶颈，是架构本身。

我们用来评估模型的 benchmark，和我们训练模型用的任务本质上是一回事，就像同一枚硬币的两面。但真实世界的分布和任务要混乱得多、模糊得多、差异大得多。

我们是，我们是那个，人类与 LLM 的混合体现在非常非常成功，但脱离人类的 LLM，还远远不是那么回事。完全不是。

对我来说，AGI 是一个能在没有任何人类介入的情况下自我改进的模型。

我们整个团队去度假，看看实验室一周能不能自己产出更好的东西。……然后把假期延长两倍、四倍，直到永久放假。

English

Jerry Tworek, the former OpenAI VP who ran the Strawberry and reasoning teams, and Rohan Anil, one of Gemini's four pretraining leads who came from Google Brain and then Anthropic, have co-founded Core Automation, one of SF's hottest new labs. Their starting premise is deliberately contrarian: 'The first step to replacing transformers is appreciating deeply how far they were able to carry us.' Both pretraining and RL at scale have been 'mastered'; the bottleneck now is the architecture itself.

The deeper argument: models are trained in the lab but deployed in the real world, and benchmarks have converged with training data while real-world tasks stay messier. Jerry, who once predicted AGI by 2025, says scaling RL didn't solve real-world tasks. The answer, in his view, is models that learn at test time on users' data: in-context learning is too shallow (roughly 20 minutes of Codex usage before compaction), fine-tuning suffers catastrophic forgetting, so you need an architecture that can express learning itself.
